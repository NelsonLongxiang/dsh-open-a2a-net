# superagent: asking another team, on an approved card

Status: agreed with the owner on 2026-11-11. The owner asked for the channel: 「加一个受控的对外通道……由主机代为发出，对方的回执再作为跟进消息回到原工单」. The owner also asked that 「需要允许 superagent 成员审批角色可以帮忙审批」.

## Context

A team session holds one team role. It sits in no other canvas, and its sessions neither send nor receive A2A work themselves (role-tools; `[superagent-team-canvas]`). Other teams reach a superagent team at its canvas address, but the team itself had no way to ask another team for anything. A member that needed another team's help could only hand the ticket back, or put the need to the owner, who then had to send it by hand from a session of their own.

## Decisions

- **O1 Asking.** Every team session has `a2a_outbound_request { ticket_id, address, text, why }`. The router may ask on any open ticket. A member or an observer may ask only on a ticket it holds. The address is another team's: `<zone>/<id8>`, or `<zone>/canvas/<name>`, optionally followed by `@<fp8>`. The team's own address and its own sessions are refused, and the team's A2A Source must be on, since its identity is what sends.
- **O2 A P0 card.** The request becomes a P0 decision card with source `outbound`. The card shows who asks, the ticket, the address, the exact text and the reason. Its options are `批准：以团队名义发出` and `拒绝`. P0 cannot be approved with one click. A ticket has at most three outbound requests waiting or in flight.
- **O3 Who decides.** The owner decides on the team page, as for any card. Team settings also carry `approvers`: members or observers of this team whom the owner lets decide outbound cards. The setting is owner-only, like a rule change (D10), and the router cannot be an approver. An approver decides with `a2a_decision_decide { card_id, option, reason }`. It can decide only outbound cards, never a card it proposed itself, and only with a reason. The decision records who decided.
- **O4 Sending.** On approval the host sends the approved text and nothing else. It goes from the team's A2A identity, under a header line naming the team and the ticket. It is an ordinary route, so the receiving host's admission and the receiving team's policy apply. The host records which task belongs to which ticket. If the send fails (the address is not reachable or not admitted), the failure is added to the ticket as a note. *Sender scope (owner ruling 2026-11-12):* the team's A2A identity sits in no canvas team, so it declares no scope of its own; an approved card is its scope for the card's exact address while the host sends that card, and for nothing else. Signature, deposit and the receiving host's admission still apply.
- **O5 The answer.** The other team's formal receipt on that task reaches the original ticket as a note, the same way a review verdict does. Only receipts with a trusted proof count. The task then counts as answered.

## Recovery of a retained approved send

The owner requested this repair on 2026-11-14 after preview.25 failed outbound recovery investigation. The new owner operations do not reopen or alter a decision, create another business card, or expand member/router tools. Deployment and actual business recovery are separate operations.

- **Original approval and payload.** New outbound cards keep a server-owned envelope with the ticket, target, text, team and title. Recovery checks the same retained approved decision and decision timestamp (30-day retention), proposer, exact payload and current canvas; the original ticket must remain open and its A2A Source enabled and unpaused. Missing, declined, changed or expired approval refuses recovery.
- **Before approval is consumed.** If the A2A Source is disabled or the team is paused, approving an outbound card is refused before persisting the decision. The same card stays pending across restart and can be approved once conditions are restored; no task or transport is created by that refusal.
- **Persistent attempt chain.** Each host-generated attempt task is saved before transport, linked to the original card and previous failed tasks. A preparing checkpoint is saved before identity setup; a dispatching checkpoint is saved before route. Restart never auto-resends a dispatching or unknown attempt. Short exclusive file transactions plus compare-and-set prevent stale or concurrent instances from reserving duplicate attempts. Orphan locks fail closed; this change does not automatically remove them.
- **Positive non-delivery proof.** The home forwarding implementation emits delivery:not-sent only before making any dispatch attempt. That marker is stored with the original payload fingerprint and returned by query. Remote errors are stripped of the marker. Failed status, timeout, pending, an absent/expired query or failure prose is not proof of non-delivery. Delivery and recovery share the same home-target normalization: only a canvas qualified by this home host’s owner fingerprint is bare for the local wire fingerprint; foreign-owner qualification stays intact, and the original approved address never changes. A local pre-transport preparing checkpoint is independently unsent. After dispatch preparation, retry requires a current exact not-sent query, not merely an old local flag. Completed results reconcile as sent; existing ledger debt vetoes another dispatch.
- **Formal owner actions.** POST /__dsh_a2a/superagent accepts action outbound-reconcile or outbound-retry with card_id and task_id, plus the existing teamId selection. Both actions require the outer control gate and original-request Host Connection browser-owner proof. API identity is not a substitute. Clients cannot override sender, target or text. Reconcile never dispatches or creates identity. Retry rechecks approval and sender before reserving one new attempt. A stale displayed task or concurrent click is refused.
- **UI semantics.** Ticket details show outbound state, original text, task and error. 核对原外发 only reconciles. 恢复原批准外发 is offered only for retained approval plus explicit non-delivery evidence, with confirmation and server-side revalidation. 重试工单（不重发外发） still reassigns the ticket; it is not outbound recovery. A trusted original-task receipt prevents retry, including a receipt arriving during reconciliation.
- **Authorization remains narrow.** Route uses the held team CLI identity, exact original message/target and the persisted task. An approved card temporarily supplies sender scope for that exact target only; signature, online deposit and receiver admission still apply. Durable approved attempts do not automatically renew-and-route with another generated task. Reverse reviewer admission is not outbound authority.
- **Legacy limitation.** Old failed records, including the .65 preview.25 incident, do not contain the retained envelope and durable attempts added here. This repair does not invent or migrate their proof: they remain visible, and recovery explicitly refuses with a reconciliation-required reason. Do not edit failed back to waiting, delete idempotency outcomes, use ticket retry, recreate the same batch card or manually switch transport IDs. Any legacy maintenance/resend needs authoritative reconciliation and separate authorization. No real .65 card is sent by development or tests.

Engineering regressions cover owner auth, payload-override refusal, original approval preservation, restart, concurrent instances, late receipts, unknown results and write failures. Engineering test results are not RC/spec-validator or production acceptance.
## One-request continuation for reconciled legacy failures

Owner development ruling (2026-11-15): implement a narrow original-request recovery, not a standing CLI trust relationship. Code, isolated engineering evidence and PR delivery are in scope; deployment, changes to the live sender/receiver configuration and actual business dispatch remain separate authorized operations.

- **Original group, not another business card.** The sender groups retained failed records only when ticket, target, exact text and proposer match. Every retained decided card must still explicitly approve that original message, within the existing 30-day horizon; the ticket is open and its Source is enabled and unpaused. New cards use their stored payload; pre-envelope cards require their exact retained question and decision, not an error summary, replacement text or a host note. Recovery rereads disk decisions and the outbound group, and its group CAS rejects concurrent changes. Missing or inconsistent records remain UNKNOWN.
- **Explicit historical reconciliation.** Old failed prose and absent/expired queries never become automatic not-sent proof. Preparing requires the sender browser owner's `checked_original_chain=true` and an audit statement covering the original identity, attempts and stopped old dispatch paths. Issuing the permit requires the receiver browser owner's `checked_not_admitted=true` plus its reconciliation statement; any retained old admission, ledger, delivery correspondence or idempotency claim vetoes the grant. Exact original caller/target/body fingerprints are also checked across retained task ids and both wait modes; a different transport id cannot hide an already claimed business payload. Absence after normal TTL eviction remains only absence, not non-delivery proof. If either owner cannot establish those facts, the procedure stops. The new attestation is recorded separately and does not retroactively manufacture original transport evidence or business approval.
- **One immutable continuation.** The signed sender proposal binds all original card/task ids, approval digest/time, exact wire message, target, fixed recovery task/context, caller CLI key, sender host key/home and receiver host key/home/full SessionId. The receiver signs one time-limited permit for that exact proposal (maximum one hour), after checking the current linked host and online CLI. No roster, canvas, bot-list or standing home link is created. Host Connection browser-owner proof and the outer control gate apply on both sides; API/model/member identities cannot substitute.
- **Ordered single effect.** Preparation persists the same proposal on every original card without changing old state/task/error. The sender saves a dispatch checkpoint and its owed row before network effect. The receiver performs fresh identity/authority/target/queue checks, saves its inbound row, durably consumes the permit, then invokes the existing sourced followup. An ordinary old request must recheck recovery fences after its asynchronous proof checks, in the same synchronous turn as its idempotency claim: either the existing claim vetoes the owner grant or the owner grant fences the old in-flight request before any effect, including same-payload requests with a different transport id. It confirms accepted only for the exact enqueued/claimed correspondence. Crashes or failed bookkeeping may retain UNKNOWN, never permission for a replacement POST. Parallel instances use short exclusive disk-CAS transactions; orphan locks fail closed for separately authorized maintenance.
- **Reconciliation and original-ticket result.** Repeated send calls, lost replies and restart query only the fixed continuation task/fingerprint. Only an explicit `recovery_delivery=accepted` fact means transport acceptance; it is not business completion. A trusted terminal receipt from the fixed receiver must also have the original caller-ledger settlement witness before its saved result is noted once on the original ticket. ACK/progress still holds business debt. Note failure leaves a durable pending note for the existing team pass; the note text is idempotent. Old failed records remain failed, with the separate continuation/result attached. Ordinary ticket retry and another same-business card are not alternate recovery paths.
- **Revocation, bounds and limits.** The receiver may revoke the exact request, not another caller or target. Revocation and link withdrawal prevent further authority without erasing a factual accepted outcome. Consumed/revoked request fences prevent replay of old ids or the exact original payload; they do not block unrelated principals or targets. The journal is versioned, atomically written with file fsync, limited to 128 records / 4 MiB, and rejects damage or capacity overflow. Tombstones are not silently GC'd or renewed. This first version supports an exact live joined local session target, not arbitrary canvas failover, another member, a new home/key or cold-session wake. A missing original approval, expired permit, changed identity, unresolved stage or lost journal stops the continuation for human reconciliation; no auto-repair or permission expansion is implied.

Dispatching/unknown/accepted/answered state never authorizes a second POST. The wire request carries `request_recovery` only on that exact signed async request.

## Consequences

An approver is an agent: letting it decide trades the owner's attention for speed. The owner chooses who it is, can take the role away in settings, and sees every decision with its reason. The text that leaves is the text on the card; no ticket content goes with it. A reply that arrives as a message rather than a receipt starts a thread of its own, as any message to the team address does. Remote members are not approvers: an approver must be a session on this host.
